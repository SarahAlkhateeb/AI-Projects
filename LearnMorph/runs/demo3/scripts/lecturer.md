Let me turn what you just saw into a habit you can use anywhere. I'll call it the zero test. Whenever a bar is meant to show you how big something is, you ask where the bar starts. At zero, the height is trustworthy. At 100, as in the squished drawing, that same 4 million step gets stretched until it looks like a doubling, even though the data never changed.

You can widen the test into three plain questions, and I'd say they probably cover most of what goes wrong with numbers. Compared to what, meaning is 4 million big or small next to the 104 million it sits on? Out of how many, meaning is that mean of 436,000 describing ten people or ten thousand, and how are they spread out? And who got left out, meaning who is hiding beyond the edge of the chart, like our tenth dot that ran right off the page?

Notice that none of these questions needs any algebra. They only ask you to slow down and look at the floor of the bar, the size of the crowd, and the empty seats.
